A heating setpoint for one home with an air-to-water heat pump and floor heating. It moves heat
out of the hours when electricity costs the most and keeps the room inside its comfort band.
Demand response programs add up many homes like this one. BOPTEST's own controller holds the
setpoint just above the comfort floor whatever the price.

Our own design, written for BOPTEST's `bestest_hydronic_heat_pump` (a simulated home in
Brussels, heating only, prices in euros). It is not taken from any standard or library
sequence. It keeps no state: each step's setpoint depends only on that step's inputs. The
runtime passes BOPTEST's forecasts in as inputs; every decision about them is made inside the
verified graph.

## Interface

| Point | Type | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `pri00`..`pri23` | real inputs, per kWh | Electricity price for the current hour (`pri00`) and the next 23 hours |
| `TLo00`..`TLo02` | real inputs, K | Lower comfort limit (floor) now, in 1 h and in 2 h |
| `THi00`..`THi03` | real inputs, K | Upper comfort limit (ceiling) now and in 1, 2 and 3 h |
| `TZon` | real input, K | Room temperature now |
| `TSet` | real output, K | Heating setpoint |

## Behavior in brief

- **Peak-price hours:** an hour is peak-price when its price is at least 75 % of the way from
  the day's lowest to highest price (and the day's prices differ by more than 0.01 per kWh).
- **Normal:** 0.2 K above the comfort floor, never below 20.5 °C, and already up for a floor
  rising within 2 h.
- **During a peak-price hour, coast:** drop 1.5 K below base, but never below the coming floor
  plus 0.1 K.
- **Before a peak-price hour (within 3 h), preheat:** normal plus 1 K, capped 2 K below the
  coming ceiling, and skipped if the room is already more than 0.5 K above normal.
- A peak-price hour now takes precedence over one coming.

## Validation

- 22 scenarios from the independent reference model: flat days, peaks now and coming while
  occupied and away, a peak just beyond the look-ahead, an already-warm room, dropping ceilings,
  arrivals and departures around a peak, the threshold edge, and a forecast rolling through a peak.
- A 33-block hand-built graph (using `Reals.MultiMax` and `Reals.MultiMin` over the hourly
  values) passes all 22; 21 deliberate mistakes each fail at least one.

## How it did

- Muse Spark: first try 3 of 3, 17 of 17 scenarios each (the run used the first 17; re-scoring
  the same replies against all 22 changed no verdict). An easy spec: no timers, no stored values,
  and the task states each formula. It shows the new width blocks are usable, not that the model
  is strong on hard sequences.
- The rule itself, run from `reference.py` (Python, not yet the verified graph through the
  runtime) on BOPTEST against its own controller, 14-day windows with a perfect price forecast:
  typical heating window cost −8.4 %, discomfort −14.8 %; on two windows it was not tuned on,
  cost −6.9 % and −6.0 %. Live runs of the verified graph are still to do. See
  [Runtime results](/mechanical-muse/results/runtime/#home-price-response-probe).

Prices in this building swing little (about 0.17 to 0.26 euro per kWh including fixed fees), so
the per-home saving is small; the point is the power moved out of peak-price hours.
