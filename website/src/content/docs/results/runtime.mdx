:::caution[Keep these beside the numbers]
Every BOPTEST number is a short window, compared with BOPTEST's own baseline controller over the
same window, and BOPTEST computes the KPIs; energy is HVAC energy as BOPTEST counts it. These are
results from a simulation, not field measurements.

Test case `multizone_office_complex_air` (BOPTEST v0.9.0) unless stated, 120 s steps, one-day
warm-up.

## Chilled-water plant reset with air handler requests

**2026-10-27.** Day 185 (a Monday) from 12:00, 300 steps (10 h). Three verified air handler
request sequences feed the verified plant reset.

| Over the 10 h window | Baseline | Plant reset with requests | Change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| BOPTEST `ener_tot` (kWh/m², whole building) | 0.3255 | 0.3101 | −4.7% |
| BOPTEST `cost_tot` | 0.0227 | 0.0213 | −6.2% |
| BOPTEST `emis_tot` | 0.0930 | 0.0879 | −5.6% |
| BOPTEST `tdis_tot` (thermal discomfort, K·h/zone) | 0.1534 | 0.1564 | +2.0% |
| Chiller energy (kWh, from 120 s power samples) | 4,710 | 4,042 | −14.2% |
| CHW pump energy (kWh, same method) | 2,300 | 2,352 | +2.2% |

What it does not show: one summer afternoon and evening; thermal discomfort rose 2 %; the
pressure half saved no pump energy (the saving is at the chillers).

**Muse Spark's drafts, 2026-10-27.** Two plant reset graphs drafted by Muse Spark (one from the
recorded session, one from a live session) ran in place of our hand-built graph over the same
window. Both gave setpoint writes and KPIs identical to the hand-built graph's run (`ener_tot`
0.3101). That is identical behavior on this window, not equivalence in general.

**First live run, 2026-10-26.** Day 185 from 05:30, 4 hours, request count fixed at 0: energy
−5.4 %, chiller energy −18 %, thermal discomfort no worse. One summer morning, not a season.

## Fail-safe, live

**2026-10-27.** Day 186 from 00:00, 60 steps, with floor 1's supply air temperature replaced by
NaN for steps 20 to 24 (a labelled test fault). On each faulted step the reading showed as no
data, the floor 1 request sequence and the plant reset went degraded, and both chilled-water
setpoints were handed back to BOPTEST's own controllers. From step 25 the reset wrote again.

## Duct static pressure reset

**2026-10-27.** Same 10 h window, adding 15 zone request sequences and a static pressure reset
per floor.

| Over the 10 h window, vs BOPTEST's baseline | Chilled-water reset only | + static pressure v1 | + static pressure v2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| BOPTEST `ener_tot` | −4.7% | −33.6% | −24.1% |
| BOPTEST `cost_tot` | −6.2% | −34.1% | −24.2% |
| BOPTEST `emis_tot` | −5.6% | −33.9% | −24.2% |
| BOPTEST `tdis_tot` (thermal discomfort) | +2.0% | +8.9% | −42.1% |
| BOPTEST `idis_tot` (CO2 discomfort) | no change | +51.4% | +17.4% |

Version 1 starved the zones: no zone ever asked, so the reset trimmed to 50 Pa and saved energy
by under-ventilating. Version 2 asks from the airflow shortfall and settled near 165 to 215 Pa.
What it does not show: one afternoon, and v2's 90 % level was chosen after seeing v1 fail on this
same window; v2 still costs CO2 comfort.

## Bring your own sequence: revision B

**2026-10-27.** The workbench's sample sheet (an AHU supply-air temperature reset), read from a
photo, drafted, bound and run on three floors beside our own sequences. Revision A's humidity
thresholds latched its override on, so its reset never acted. Revision B raised them.

| Day 143, 06:00, 8 h, against baseline | Energy | Cost | Emissions | Thermal discomfort |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Our sequences alone | −3.3 % | −5.3 % | −4.3 % | +0.5 % |
| + revision A | −0.7 % | −3.4 % | −2.1 % | +6.7 % |
| + revision B | −37.3 % | −27.9 % | −32.5 % | −10.7 % |

Revision B's thresholds were chosen after revision A failed on day 143, so it was then run on
five windows it was not tuned on:

The saving is mostly boiler gas: a warmer supply air needs far less reheat at the zones. In
summer the outdoor air is above the reset band, so the sheet's reset adds nothing, and on day 199
it did slightly worse than our sequences alone. Single windows in simulation.

## What a fault costs

**2026-10-27.** Day 185 from noon, 10 h, floor 1's supply-air reading biased 4 K high for 2 h,
against the same run without the fault: +130 kWh, +44 kgCO2e and +$12.25, nearly all chiller
electricity, about a fifth of the ten hours' saving. One window, one fault. See the
[Emissions ledger](/mechanical-muse/how-it-works/emissions/).

## Home price response, live

**2026-10-28.** The verified graph through `mm-runtime` (`building: home`) on BOPTEST's heat pump
house, spot prices, two weeks of 15-minute steps after a week of warm-up, against BOPTEST's own
controller. Each step the runtime passes BOPTEST's forecast in as readings; the graph decides.

| Window | Cost | Energy | Thermal discomfort | Power, most expensive quarter of prices | BOPTEST peak demand KPI |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Typical heating (from day 108) | −8.4 % | −7.7 % | −14.8 % | 1.17 → 0.87 kW (−26 %) | +16.4 % |
| Peak heating (from day 16) | −8.3 % | −7.5 % | −81.9 % | 1.63 → 1.03 kW (−37 %) | +3.7 % |

:::caution[Read these beside the numbers]
Every setpoint matched the independent reference model; no fail-safe event. The peak-price
power is ours, from recorded readings, not a BOPTEST KPI. The single highest 15-minute draw
rose: shifting heat concentrates it before the expensive hours, and homes on the same prices
would add those rebounds up. Perfect forecast, one house, heating only.
:::

## Home price response probe

**2026-10-28.** BOPTEST's `bestest_hydronic_heat_pump` (a home in Brussels), highly dynamic
price, 14-day windows, against BOPTEST's own controller. The rule was run from the
spec's Python reference model, **not yet the verified graph through the runtime**, with a
perfect price forecast.

| Window | Cost | Thermal discomfort | Mean power in the most expensive quarter of 15-min prices |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Typical heating (BOPTEST window) | −8.4 % | −14.8 % | 1.17 → 0.85 kW |
| Peak heating (BOPTEST window) | −8.3 % | −81.9 % | 1.68 → 1.04 kW |
| From day 40 (not tuned on) | −6.9 % | −45.2 % | 1.49 → 0.94 kW |
| From day 315 (not tuned on) | −6.0 % | −71.5 % | 1.62 → 1.24 kW |

Energy fell 5.7 to 7.7 %, so part of the cost cut is plain saving. Prices in this building swing
little, so the per-home saving is small.
